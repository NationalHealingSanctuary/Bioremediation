# Phytostimulation & Rhizodegradation v1.0 Protocol

_A Subterranean Biological Reactor Framework for Managed Contaminant Degradation_

## 1. Executive Summary & Managed Uptake Mass-Balance

The **NHS Rhizodegradation Protocol (v1.0)** establishes an advanced biological framework designed to degrade total petroleum hydrocarbons (TPH), BTEX compounds, PAHs, and organic solvents directly within the rhizosphere. In field-scale bioremediation, claiming absolute zero plant uptake is biologically unrealistic due to passive apoplastic water transport across root tissues. This protocol addresses that physiological reality through a **Rhizosphere-First & Managed Translocation Strategy**.

**Core Design Mandate: Rhizosphere-First Degradation & Low Translocation (TF ≪ 1.0)**

Over 90% of contaminant breakdown occurs externally in soil pore water via root-stimulated microbial and enzymatic catalysis before organic pollutants enter root cells. For the minor fraction passively absorbed during transpirational water pull, translocation to above-ground foliage is strictly suppressed (TF ≪ 1.0). As a result, foliar concentrations stay far below hazardous thresholds, allowing trimmings to remain clean organic material that can be safely managed through long-interval coppicing rather than high-cost hazardous waste disposal.

## 2. The Four Subterranean Degradation Pillars

### Pillar 1: Root Exudate & Allelochemical Catalysis

Plant roots continuously exude organic acids (citrate, malate, oxalate), amino acids, phenolics, and flavonoids into soil pores. These exudates serve as energy substrates and chemoattractants that expand specialized hydrocarbonoclastic bacterial populations (Pseudomonas, Bacillus, Rhodococcus) by 10x to 100x compared to bulk soil.

### Pillar 2: Radial Oxygen Loss (ROL) Aerobic Micro-Zones

Halophytic and wetland root networks transport atmospheric oxygen down into anoxic subsurface sediments. Oxygen diffusing outward from root tips establishes oxygenated rhizosphere micro-zones, fueling aerobic ring-cleavage enzymes (monooxygenases and dioxygenases) essential for breaking down benzene, toluene, and PAHs.

### Pillar 3: Extracellular Enzymatic Cleavage (Co-Metabolism)

Plant roots and associated rhizosphere microbes exude non-specific extracellular enzymes—including peroxidases, laccases, and esterases—directly into soil pore water. These enzymes initiate extracellular cleavage of heavy petroleum fractions, mineralizing hydrophobic contaminants into harmless water, carbon dioxide, and humic soil complexes.

### Pillar 4: Mycorrhizal Hyphosphere Trapping & Bio-Filtration

Symbiotic Arbuscular Mycorrhizal Fungi (AMF, Glomeromycota) extend fungal hyphae deep into soil pores, expanding the catalytic surface area by 100x to 1,000x. Hyphae secrete glomalin to stabilize soil aggregates while physically binding hydrophobic hydrocarbon molecules in the hyphosphere for microbial breakdown.

## 4. Operational Management & Biosecurity

* **Low-Frequency Long-Interval Maintenance:** Because primary degradation occurs in the soil matrix and foliar translocation is minimal (TF ≪ 1.0), biomass does not accumulate hazardous contaminant levels. Harvesting is reduced to long-interval structural pruning and canopy management every few years.
* **Zero-Fruiting Control:** Pre-anthesis mechanical coppicing ('chop & drop') maintains plants in a perpetual juvenile vegetative state, completely preventing seed or fruit production over contaminated horizons and protecting local wildlife.
* **Subterranean Biological Rebar:** Living root systems remain intact indefinitely, stabilizing slope soils, preventing erosion, and maintaining deep oxygen pathways during storm events.
