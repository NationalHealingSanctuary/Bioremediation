## **1. Executive Summary & Scientific Rationale**

\*\*At a Glance — What This Protocol Does\*\*

This protocol uses native tropical fungi as living filtration systems along bioswales and drainage channels. Fungal enzymes chop complex fuel and chemical spills into harmless carbon dioxide and water, while subterranean root-fungal networks lock heavy metals deep underground so they cannot contaminate local water supplies or enter the food chain.

The NHS Mycoremediation & Subsurface Fungal Barrier Protocol v1.0 defines a passive, non-chemical biological treatment architecture engineered to mineralize recalcitrant petroleum hydrocarbons, filter biological pathogens, and immobilize heavy metals within soil and pore-water matrices. Rather than relying on synthetic biostimulants, this system leverages the extracellular oxidative machinery of native Caribbean basidiomycete saprophytes and symbiotic arbuscular mycorrhizal fungi (AMF — Glomeromycota).

### **Primary Biochemical & Enzymatic Pathways**

* **Extracellular Ligninolytic Cleavage:** Fungal mycelia secrete non-specific oxidative enzymes—specifically laccases, lignin peroxidases (LiP), manganese peroxidases (MnP), and cutinases/esterases. These enzymes generate highly reactive free radicals that uncouple complex aromatic rings, polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs), total petroleum hydrocarbons (TPH), and synthetic polymer linkages directly within soil pore spaces.
* **Subterranean Heavy Metal Sequestration:** Concurrently, arbuscular mycorrhizal networks sequester heavy metal cations (Pb²⁺, Cd²⁺, Cu²⁺) within subterranean fungal tissues while exuding Glomalin-Related Soil Proteins (GRSP). These insoluble glycoproteins chelate toxic metal ions and bind soil macro-aggregates, physically restricting contaminant transport through the subterranean root zone.

### **Mathematical Kinetics & Trophic Accumulation Equations**

\*\*Contaminant Kinetics Equations & Operational Meaning\*\*

\*\*1. Bioconcentration Factor (BCF):\
\*\*BCF = C\_fungus / C\_substrate\
Operational Translation: Quantifies metal uptake from surrounding soil/water into fungal biomass. A BCF > 1.0 indicates active bioaccumulation.

\*\*2. Translocation Factor (TF):\
\*\*TF = C\_fruiting\_body / C\_mycelium\
Operational Translation: Measures whether metals travel upward into the mushroom cap. A TF << 1.0 defines Subterranean Excluders (locking metals underground permanently with zero harvest needed). A TF > 1.0 defines Cap Hyperaccumulators, which require mandatory surface cap removal and high-temperature incineration.

\*\*CONTAMINATED MATERIAL DISPOSAL RULE (WASTE STREAM STANDARD)\*\*

Harvested hyperaccumulated surface biomass (where Translocation Factor TF > 1.0) must undergo controlled high-temperature incineration or be routed directly to hazardous waste containment. Under no circumstances may hyperaccumulated cap biomass be composted on-site or left to decompose, preventing the re-mobilization of heavy metals into Grand Cayman's fragile freshwater lens and coastal water table.

## **2. Grand Cayman Native Fungal Asset Library & Global Safety Standards**

This species fleet is selected specifically for Grand Cayman's hydrogeological environment: karstic limestone geology, hypersaline mangrove estuarine margins, thin tropical alkaline soils, intense UV radiation, and year-round maritime temperatures (25–40°C). This protocol and its associated fungal fleet are strictly non-transferable and prohibited from unregulated release into continental ecosystems without local biosecurity approval.

### **Dual-Layer Global Biosafety & Biosecurity Framework**

* **Standard 1 — Human Health Safety (CDC/NIH BMBL RG1 / BSL-1):** Governed by CDC/NIH criteria. All fleet species belong to Risk Group 1 (RG1 / BSL-1), posing minimal hazard to healthy adults, field crews, or local communities.
* **Standard 2 — Phytosanitary Release Clearance (EPPO PM 6 / IPPC ISPM 11):** Establishes a 2-tier clearance: Tier 1 (Unrestricted Open-Swale Release for native, non-pathogenic saprophytes/symbionts) vs. Tier 2 (Restricted / Ex-Situ Contained Bioreactors Only for opportunistic endophytes like Pestalotiopsis microspora to protect local agriculture from leaf spot).

### **Fungal Fleet Catalog & Functional Profiles**

* **Pink Oyster (Pleurotus djamor): \[CDC/NIH RG1 / BSL-1 | EPPO Tier 1 (Unrestricted Open-Swale)] —** Breaks down TPH, diesel, PAHs, and absorbs Pb²⁺, Cd²⁺, Cr³⁺ in warm tropical soils. High affinity for coconut coir and sugarcane bagasse carriers.
* **Tropical Turkey Tail (Trametes villosa): \[CDC/NIH RG1 / BSL-1 | EPPO Tier 1 (Unrestricted Open-Swale)] —** Exudes high-potency laccase, LiP, and MnP to cleave benzene rings, POPs, PCBs, and pesticides. Forms dense subterranean hyphal mats (TF < 0.2).
* **Mangrove White-Rot Fungus (Flavodon flavus): \[CDC/NIH RG1 / BSL-1 | EPPO Tier 1 (Unrestricted Coastal Release)] —** Euhalophytic marine fungus functioning in full seawater (up to 50 ppt NaCl). Produces halotolerant MnP to mineralize toxic phenols and petroleum in coastal mangrove swales.
* **Tropical Cinnabar Polypore (Pycnoporus sanguineus): \[CDC/NIH RG1 / BSL-1 | EPPO Tier 1 (Unrestricted Open-Swale)] —** Operates at peak laccase activity under tropical heat (30–40°C optimum). Oxidizes crude oil fractions and phenols while biosorbing Cu²⁺ and Pb²⁺.
* **Phoenix Oyster (Pleurotus pulmonarius): \[CDC/NIH RG1 / BSL-1 | EPPO Tier 1 (Unrestricted Open-Swale)] —** Immobilized biomass in alginate matrices adsorbs Hg²⁺, Cd²⁺, and Cu²⁺ in sumps while degrading herbicides like atrazine.
* **Arbuscular Mycorrhizal Fungi (Rhizophagus irregularis): \[CDC/NIH RG1 / BSL-1 | EPPO Tier 1 (Unrestricted Rhizosphere Inoculation)] —** Forms mutualisms with pioneer plants. Sequesters heavy metals in mycorrhizal root tissues, secretes glomalin to stabilize soil aggregates, and mitigates Na⁺ salt stress.
* **Caribbean Jelly Ear (Auricularia cornea): \[CDC/NIH RG1 / BSL-1 | EPPO Tier 1 (Unrestricted Swale/Mulch Broadcast)] —** Deployed in aerated mulch benches. Hydrated mycelial tissue swells during rain, physically trapping suspended metal particulates (Pb, Cd, Zn) and silt.
* **Cuban Bracket (Trametes cubensis): \[CDC/NIH RG1 / BSL-1 | EPPO Tier 1 (Unrestricted Embankment Release)] —** Reinforces loose fill along swale embankments against storm erosion while exuding laccases to oxidize fuel residues and surface hydrocarbons.
* **Tropical Reishi (Ganoderma lucidum / G. applanatum): \[CDC/NIH RG1 / BSL-1 | EPPO Tier 1 (Unrestricted Biopile/Barrier Placement)] —** Chitin-rich cell walls provide expansive biosorption area for hexavalent chromium (Cr⁶⁺) and lead (Pb²⁺), showing high tolerance to landfill leachate shocks.
* **Neotropical Hydrolase Specialist (Pestalotiopsis microspora): \[CDC/NIH RG1 / BSL-1 | EPPO Tier 2 (Restricted Ex-Situ Contained Bioreactor Only)] —** \[EX-SITU CONTAINER USE ONLY] Secretes serine esterases and cutinases that hydrolyze polyester polyurethane (PUR) plastics under controlled conditions. Prohibited from open-field broadcast.

## **3. Evaluation Framework, Edaphic Boundaries & Operational Triggers**

Fungal enzymes require specific environmental operating windows. When field conditions exceed these boundaries, specific maintenance rules are triggered to restore optimal biological treatment.

### **Actionable Maintenance Trigger Rules**

**Trigger Rule 1 (pH Re-Buffering Standard):** If bed pH exceeds 6.8 → Top-dress filter beds with acidic organic mulches, elemental sulfur, or biochar to reset local micro-acidic conditions (pH 4.5–6.5) and keep peroxidase enzymes active against surrounding limestone karst.

**Trigger Rule 2 (Drainage & Redox Control Standard):** If standing water persists past 48 hours or Redox Potential (Eh) drops below +150 mV → Execute mechanical gravel aeration clearing or French-drain flushing to restore oxygenated pore space.

**Trigger Rule 3 (Post-Surge Salinity Management Standard):** If post-storm seawater surge drives inland swale Electrical Conductivity (EC) above 35 mS/cm (25 ppt) → Perform a freshwater wash-down cycle to flush excess sodium from non-halotolerant fungal matrices.

**Trigger Rule 4 (Hyperaccumulated Waste Stream Rule):** If cap hyperaccumulating fungi (TF > 1.0) are used → Execute mandatory cap harvesting and high-temperature incineration to prevent metal re-mobilization into the island's water table.

## **4. Field Auditing & Stormwater Monitoring Standards**

Field efficacy is monitored using a reliable 3-Window Storm Auditing Standard following rainfall events, eliminating the need for delicate electronic telemetry in tropical field conditions.

## **5. Non-Technical Glossary & Reference Index**

* **AMF (Arbuscular Mycorrhizal Fungi):** Beneficial soil fungi that link with plant roots to exchange nutrients and lock heavy metals in root tissues.
* **BCF (Bioconcentration Factor):** A mathematical ratio showing how effectively a fungus absorbs contaminants from surrounding soil or water into its tissue.
* **EC (Electrical Conductivity):** A measurement of salt content in soil or water, used to detect seawater storm surges in bioswales.
* **Eh (Redox Potential):** A measure of soil oxygenation; high Eh values indicate oxygenated conditions necessary for fungal enzymes to break down pollutants.
* **EPPO / IPPC:** International plant protection bodies that set biosecurity rules for releasing biological organisms into the environment.
* **GRSP (Glomalin-Related Soil Proteins):** Sticky, long-lasting glycoproteins produced by mycorrhizal fungi that bind soil particles and trap toxic metal ions.
* **Ligninolytic Enzymes:** Powerful oxidative enzymes (laccase, MnP, LiP) that break down tough organic materials, fuels, and synthetic chemicals.
* **TF (Translocation Factor):** A ratio indicating whether absorbed metals stay in underground roots/hyphae (TF << 1.0) or travel up into mushroom caps (TF > 1.0).
* **VWC (Volumetric Water Content):** The percentage of water contained within soil pores; maintained between 50%–70% for optimal fungal health.
