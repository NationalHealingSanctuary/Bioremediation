## 0. Quick-Start Guide & Protocol Scope

This technical specification establishes a universal, plant-agnostic Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) for field crews implementing phytostimulation and rhizodegradation across organic-contaminated horizons, brownfield sectors, and landfill perimeters. By separating standardized operational workflows from individual botanical profiles, field teams apply a consistent engineering process regardless of which specific plant asset is selected from the Master Plant Canvas.

### Master Execution Sequence

{% stepper %}
{% step %}
#### Conduct Baseline Site Characterization

Analyze soil pH, electrical conductivity (EC mS/cm), groundwater depth, and target organic contaminant classes (e.g., TPH, DRO/GRO, BTEX, PAHs, Sodic Runoff) across designated sector grids.
{% endstep %}

{% step %}

Query the Master Plant Canvas (master-plant-database.xlsx) to match site edaphic conditions and contaminant targets with specific plant species profiles, growth rates, and salt tolerance limits.

Perform PGPR bacterial slurry root dipping during transplantation, execute volumetric hydro-priming water scaling rules, and enforce quarterly TPH environmental performance auditing.
{% endstep %}

### 0.1 Technical Glossary of Bioremediation Terminology

* **Rhizodegradation:** The biological breakdown of organic contaminants (fuels, solvents, PAHs) within the plant root zone, driven by root exudates and active rhizosphere bacterial metabolism.
* **Phytostimulation / Root Exudates:** The continuous secretion of organic acids, carbohydrates, amino acids, and signal compounds by plant roots to stimulate bacterial population density and metabolic activity in surrounding soil.
* **PGPR (Plant Growth-Promoting Rhizobacteria):** Beneficial soil bacteria (Pseudomonas, Bacillus, Rhodococcus, Azospirillum) living around root systems that degrade organic pollutants, synthesize ACC deaminase, and enhance plant stress tolerance.
* **Translocation Factor (TF):** The ratio of contaminant concentration in above-ground foliage to below-ground root tissue. Values < 1.0 indicate strict root immobilization (excluder behavior).
* **Bioconcentration Factor (BCF):** The ratio of contaminant concentration in plant root tissues relative to the surrounding soil substrate.

## 1. Master Plant Canvas Integration & Functional Selection Rules

To maintain a clean, plant-agnostic field protocol, species-specific botanical profiles, growth rates, salt tolerance thresholds, and nursery stock parameters are housed in the Master Plant Canvas (master-plant-database.xlsx). Practitioners navigate from site characterization to canvas data using three core functional selection rules:

* **Rule 1 (Contaminant Target Matching) —** Filter the Master Plant Canvas by primary contaminant class (e.g., Aliphatic Alkanes C10–C36, Diesel, BTEX, PAHs, Sodic Runoff, Organic Leachate) to identify corresponding plant functional guilds.
* **Rule 2 (Edaphic Boundary Verification) —** Cross-reference site testing data (pH, salinity EC mS/cm, water table depth) against canvas parameters to ensure plant survival and exudation efficiency under field stress.
* **Rule 3 (Mandatory Seed-Grown Stock for Canopy Trees) —** All deep-root canopy tree assets must be grown directly from seed to preserve the primary gravitropic taproot architecture for hurricane windthrow resistance. Clones and cuttings are strictly prohibited for canopy stock.

## 2. Universal Biological Treatment Pathways & Rhizosphere Dynamics

Phytostimulation operates through subterranean plant-microbe interactions that convert toxic organic pollutants into harmless carbon dioxide, water, and microbial biomass. Field crews must distinguish organic mineralization pathways from inorganic metal immobilization pathways:

* **Pathway A (Organic Contaminant Breakdown via Phytostimulation) —** Living plant roots exude organic carbon, amino acids, and signal molecules into the rhizosphere → Exudates fuel exponential growth of PGPR bacteria (Pseudomonas, Bacillus, Rhodococcus, Azospirillum) → Microbes excrete extracellular peroxidases, dioxygenases, and ring-cleaving enzymes → Organic hydrocarbons (TPH, BTEX, PAHs) are mineralized into harmless CO2, H2O, and biomass.
* **Pathway B (Subterranean Heavy Metal Lock via Phytostabilization) —** Inorganic heavy metals (Pb, Cd, Cr, As) cannot be mineralized → Plant root cell-wall mucilage, carboxylates, and phytochelatins bind metal cations directly within root tissue vacuoles and subterranean soil aggregates → Enforces strict TF < 1.0 rule, keeping heavy metals permanently locked underground.

## 3. Universal Substrate Preparation & Decompaction Protocol

Compacted brownfields, unlined landfill caps, and weathered industrial horizons restrict root penetration and inhibit rhizosphere aeration. Field crews execute substrate conditioning prior to out-planting using a standardized 3-step workflow:

{% stepper %}
{% step %}
#### Mechanical Ripping & Aeration

Mechanically rip or decompact compacted surface soils to a depth of 45–60 cm using low-impact tracked machinery. Avoid over-tilling that destroys natural soil aggregate structure.
{% endstep %}

{% step %}
#### Organic Amendment & Porosity

Incorporate coarse organic compost or fine-milled biochar at 5–10% by volume into the upper 30 cm layer to increase porosity, organic carbon content, and moisture retention.
{% endstep %}

{% step %}
#### pH & Chemistry Balancing

Test baseline soil pH across all planting sectors. If pH is below 6.0, apply agricultural lime (calcium carbonate) to buffer acidity. If pH is above 8.5, apply elemental sulfur or acidic compost to adjust pH into the optimal 6.0–8.0 window.
{% endstep %}
{% endstepper %}

## 4. Universal Transplant Bio-Inoculation & Out-Planting SOP

Pre-inoculating seedling root systems during transplantation is the single most critical operational step in establishing a functional rhizodegradation system. Direct out-planting into contaminated substrates without inoculation causes severe transplant shock and high mortality. Crews follow a 5-step SOP:

{% stepper %}
{% step %}
#### Slurry Water Preparation

Fill a clean 200-liter mixing drum with non-chlorinated rainwater or settled site water.
{% endstep %}

{% step %}
#### PGPR Bacterial Inoculation

Incorporate liquid PGPR bacterial bio-inoculant concentrate (Bacillus, Pseudomonas, Rhodococcus, and Azospirillum strains) mixed with organic humic carriers.
{% endstep %}

{% step %}
#### Root Slurry Dipping

Carefully extract nursery seedling root balls from container trays. Submerge the complete root ball into the agitated slurry for 10–15 seconds to ensure complete root coverage.
{% endstep %}

{% step %}
#### Placement & Backfill

Place the inoculated root ball into the pre-dug planting pit. Backfill the pit with decompacted soil mixed with 10% organic compost, firming gently to eliminate air pockets around the root crown.
{% endstep %}

{% step %}
#### Volumetric Hydro-Priming Rule

Apply initial hydro-priming water directly to the base of each newly planted seedling.

* **Small nursery plugs and 1-gallon stock:** 2.5–5.0 L per plant.
* **Deep-taproot tree stock and broadcast bed applications:** 5.0–10.0 L per plant.
{% endstep %}
{% endstepper %}

## 6. Plant Sourcing, Taproot Architecture & Biosecurity Controls

Field execution must strictly enforce plant sourcing protocols to ensure long-term structural stability and prevent genetic contamination:

{% stepper %}
{% step %}
#### Native Preference

Prioritize regional wild-type native plant species harvested from local provenances. Local native species exhibit superior climate resilience and preserve regional ecological harmony.
{% endstep %}

{% step %}
#### Mandatory Seed-Grown Stock & Taproot Protection

Clones and vegetative cuttings must NOT be used under any circumstances for canopy trees. All tree assets must be grown directly from seed. Propagating plants directly from seed preserves the primary gravitropic taproot architecture. Clones fail to form deep taproots, leaving trees structurally unstable and vulnerable to uprooting during severe tropical storms and cyclones.
{% endstep %}

{% step %}
#### IVM Registration

Any non-native or introduced species selected to fill specific extreme environmental niches must be formally registered on the Integrated Vegetation Management (IVM) protocol and subjected to quarterly risk audits.
{% endstep %}
{% endstepper %}

## 7. Step-by-Step Field Audit & Environmental Monitoring Workflow

To verify ongoing rhizodegradation performance and site biosecurity, field teams execute a quarterly monitoring checklist:

{% stepper %}
{% step %}
#### Quarterly Soil Core Sampling

Collect multi-depth soil core samples (0–15 cm, 15–45 cm, 45–90 cm) across all planting sectors and analyze for Total Petroleum Hydrocarbon (TPH) fractions and PAH concentrations via laboratory GC-MS testing.
{% endstep %}

{% step %}
#### pH & Chemistry Audits

Inspect surface soil pH and maintain within the 6.0–8.0 window. Re-apply organic compost or agricultural lime if pH drifts outside boundary limits.
{% endstep %}

{% step %}
#### Vegetation Health Checks

Conduct visual health inspections of established vegetation, auditing leaf canopy density, lateral root extension, and signs of nutrient deficiency or toxic stress.
{% endstep %}

{% step %}
#### Trail & Biosecurity Audits

Inspect perimeter security fencing, living thorny buffer zones, and mapped transit trail blocks to verify zero breaches by synanthropic animals or unauthorized humans.
{% endstep %}

{% step %}
#### IVM Compliance Audits

Audit non-native species beds under the Integrated Vegetation Management (IVM) protocol, pruning any unintended seed heads and verifying zero invasive spread beyond site boundaries.
{% endstep %}
{% endstepper %}
