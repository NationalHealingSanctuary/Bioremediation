# nhs phytodesalination protocol v1

_A Universal, Non-Harvest Biological Architecture for Managing Saline Leachate & Sodic Soils_

## 1. Executive Summary & Mass-Balance Architecture

Conventional phytoremediation relies heavily on hyperaccumulating vegetation that absorbs dissolved sodium (Na⁺), chloride (Cl⁻), and heavy metal ions from contaminated substrates and translocates them into shoot and foliar tissues (Translocation Factor, TF > 1.0). In industrial, landfill, and agricultural settings, this reliance creates a severe operational **mass-balance trap**:

* **Secondary Hazardous Waste Generation:** Sites require continuous, high-cost harvesting, transport, and thermal incineration of tons of toxic foliage every season.
* **Trophic Vector Exposure & Re-Contamination:** Unharvested toxic foliage senesces and redeposits accumulated solutes back onto surface topsoil horizons or is consumed by foraging avifauna and terrestrial herbivores, transferring bioaccumulated contaminants into local food webs.
* **High Operational Overhead:** It converts a natural biological containment system into an expensive, high-maintenance agricultural cleanup operation.

{% hint style="info" %}
#### ZERO-HARVEST & LOW-FREQUENCY MAINTENANCE MANDATE

This protocol eliminates secondary hazardous waste streams by prioritizing evergreen, nondeciduous halophytic species operating under strict Low Translocation Factor parameters (TF < 1.0, and TF ≪ 0.2 for endodermal excluders).

By selecting perennial species that hold living biomass intact year-round, the system avoids annual leaf drop, preventing seasonal re-contamination of topsoil horizons.

Harvesting is shifted from high-frequency farming (every 6–8 weeks) to low-frequency long-interval maintenance (pruning/coppicing every few years). Because foliage remains clean (TF < 1.0), trimmings constitute clean organic material that can be safely dropped in situ (“Chop & Drop”) to build topsoil rather than hauled away as hazardous waste.

## 2. The Four Functional Halophytic Guilds

### Guild A: Endodermal Root Ultrafiltration & Suberin Excluders (TF ≪ 0.2)

_In Simple Terms: Underground reverse-osmosis filtering._

Operating directly at the soil-root interface, an ultra-dense layer of suberin and lignin deposited within the radial cell walls of the root endodermis (the Casparian strip) establishes a hydrophobic apoplastic barrier. High negative transpirational water potential (Ψ\_leaf ≈ -3.0 to -6.0 MPa) pulls soil water through noncharged aquaporin channel proteins, physically size-excluding dissolved Na⁺, Cl⁻, and heavy metal cations (Pb²⁺, Cd²⁺). Plasma membrane H⁺-ATPase proton pumps export hydrogen ions to maintain an inside-negative membrane potential (-120 to -200 mV) that repels toxic cations. Because 90% to 99% of dissolved solutes are rejected at the root boundary, ascending xylem sap is virtually fresh water (TF ≪ 0.2), ensuring natural leaf drop decomposes into clean topsoil rather than hazardous waste.

### Guild B: Epicuticular Active Salt Gland Excreters (Internal TF < 1.0)

_In Simple Terms: Self-washing leaf surfaces._

Dissolved ions pass into the root vascular cylinder but are actively pumped out of living mesophyll cytosol by mitochondria-dense bicellular or multicellular micro-glands embedded across the leaf epidermis. Plasma membrane SOS1 Na⁺/H antiporters push excess ions out through cuticular micropores. Solar⁺ radiation evaporates the aqueous phase, leaving dry, harmless white NaCl crystals on the leaf exterior. Ambient dew and precipitation wash these epicuticular salt crusts off leaf surfaces into Keyline drainage swales, carrying dissolved ions toward saline receiving basins. Because ions are expelled onto the outer cuticle rather than accumulated inside metabolic cytosol, internal toxicity remains near zero (TF < 1.0), eliminating hazardous bioaccumulation in foraging fauna without requiring plant harvesting.

### Guild C: Tonoplast Vacuolar Sequestraters in Perennial Mats (TF < 1.0)

_In Simple Terms: Living succulent lockboxes._

Active tonoplast transport enzymes (V-ATPase and V-PPase) generate a proton gradient driving NHX1 Na⁺/H antiporters to pump sodium and chloride into large central vacuoles of succulent mesophyll cells. ⁺ Cytosolic volume is protected from osmotic shock by synthesizing compatible organic osmolytes (proline, glycine betaine, pinitol). Utilizing perennial, non-deciduous halophytic groundcovers prevents seasonal leaf drop from re-releasing salt into topsoil. The salt remains locked inside living succulent leaf vacuoles indefinitely while forming an evergreen soil shield over the site.

### Guild D: Subterranean AMF Vesicular Trappers & Biomineralizers (TF < 1.0)

_In Simple Terms: Underground rock factory._

Symbiotic Glomeromycota Arbuscular Mycorrhizal Fungi (AMF) colonize root cortical parenchyma, forming intraradical storage vesicles. Dissolved NaCl is imported across fungal plasma membranes and crystallized into solid NaCl micro-crystals directly inside fungal vesicles within the root cortex, physically trapping salt underground before it can enter the plant's vascular xylem. Simultaneously, ambient calcium (Ca²⁺) and sulfate (SO₄²⁻) react within the root cortical apoplast to precipitate amorphous calcium sulfate (CaSO · 2H₄ ₂O, gypsum) crystals:

Immobilizing Ca²⁺ as gypsum uncouples calcium from orthophosphate (P\_i), preventing salt-induced phosphate starvation, while buffering rhizosphere pH (5.5–8.0) and halting sulfuric acid (H₂SO₄) formation in anoxic muds.

## 4. Site Hydrogeology & Engineering Integration

1. **Salinity & Conductivity Thresholds:** The protocol is calibrated for hyper-saline leachate and sodic runoff profiles exhibiting Electrical Conductivity (EC > 13,000 µS/cm to 50,000+ µS/cm) and Total Dissolved Solids (TDS > 30,000 mg/L).
2. **Keyline Drainage & Dew-Flushing Hydraulics:** Guild B excreting species must be established along gently sloped Keyline bioswales leading to tidal or saline receiving basins, ensuring dew-washed epicuticular salt crusts are carried away via surface runoff rather than accumulating on dry unpaved topsoil.
3. **Subsurface Mycorrhizal Inoculation & Ca²⁺ Amendments:** Guild D species must be inoculated at the nursery stage with Glomeromycota spore blends (Glomus intraradices / Rhizophagus irregularis) and provided with calcium-bearing mineral substrates (such as marl, gypsum dust, or calcium carbonate) to trigger vesicular formation and subterranean gypsum biomineralization.

## 5. Physiological Growth Arrest & Biosecurity Protocols

* **The Zero-Fruiting Mandate:** To guarantee that no heavy metals or accumulated solutes migrate into terrestrial or avian food webs, all woody or tall emergent species across the site are subjected to preanthesis mechanical coppicing (“chop & drop”).
* **Perpetual Juvenile State:** Trimming shoots back to the stump before flowering holds plants in a perpetual juvenile vegetative state, permanently preventing fruit, seed, or flower production over chemical waste horizons.
* **Subterranean Biological Rebar:** Preserving living subterranean root systems underground stabilizes slope soils against erosion and storm windthrow (eliminating canopy sail effects) while maintaining Radial Oxygen Loss (ROL) to fuel rhizosphere microbial metabolic activity.
